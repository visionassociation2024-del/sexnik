Comprehensive Sexual Health & Wellness Guide

Your confidential, medically-backed resource for safe sex practices, STI detection, HIV PrEP/PEP access, and holistic intimacy health. Knowledge is your greatest protection.

Protection

1. Barrier Methods & Condom Efficacy

Consistent and correct use of male (external) or female (internal) latex or polyurethane condoms provides over 98% protection against unintended pregnancy and substantially reduces transmission of HIV, Chlamydia, Gonorrhea, and Trichomoniasis.

  • Always check expiration dates and CE/FDA safety certifications.
  • Use water-based or silicone-based lubricants with latex to prevent tearing.
  • Store condoms away from direct heat or friction (avoid wallets/gloveboxes).
Diagnostics

2. Regular STI & STD Screenings

Many common sexually transmitted infections—including Chlamydia, HPV, and early-stage Syphilis—are frequently asymptomatic. Regular diagnostic panels every 3 to 6 months are essential for sexually active individuals.

  • Annual full-panel blood and urine screenings for active adults.
  • Prompt medical consultation if unusual discharge, lesions, or pain occurs.
  • Partner notification and dual treatment to prevent recurring infections.
Medication

3. HIV Prevention: PrEP & Emergency PEP

Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) taken daily reduces the risk of acquiring HIV from sex by approximately 99%. For accidental potential exposure, Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) must be initiated within 72 hours.

  • PrEP is available via prescription through healthcare providers and community clinics.
  • PEP is a 28-day emergency course available in hospital emergency departments.
  • U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable) is scientifically proven for individuals on antiretroviral therapy.
Immunization

4. HPV & Hepatitis B Vaccines

The HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9) protects against 9 strains of Human Papillomavirus responsible for genital warts and various cancers. The Hepatitis B vaccine grants lifetime immunity against acute and chronic liver damage.

  • Recommended for adolescents and young adults up to age 45.
  • Administered in a 2 or 3-dose schedule for maximum lifetime antibody titers.
  • Consult your primary physician or public health department for low-cost clinics.
